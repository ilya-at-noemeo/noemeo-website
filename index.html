NOEMEO Closed beta

For photographers

Edit with a creative peer.

A second pair of eyes for your edits, and a way to learn why they work.

Noemeo looks at your photo the way a fellow photographer would — what’s working, what’s holding it back, what you might try next — and talks it through with you. Post-processing becomes a conversation, not a chore.

Noemeo Workspace is a desktop app for macOS (Windows support is planned). It reviews your photos and suggests edits; it never changes them. You make the edits yourself, in Lightroom Classic or whichever editor you use.

How it works

A critique you can argue with

  1. 01

    Review

    Add a photo and get a verdict on its craft and emotional impact, with a written critique: what's working, what isn't, and concrete suggestions.

  2. 02

    Talk it through

    Ask why, push back, or explain what you were going for. Noemeo takes what you tell it into account, and you can see and remove anything it remembers about a photo.

  3. 03

    Edit and compare

    Bring in a new version and ask whether it's better. Noemeo compares it against what came before instead of judging it from scratch.

A real session

Córdoba: better, or too much?

Noemeo’s first read pointed out what the photographer hadn’t noticed: the bright windows at the bottom pull the eye away from the dome. The photographer darkened them and asked, “Is it better, or is it too much?” Noemeo measured the change and found the mask had reached beyond the windows:

“The lower central vault is about 15 L* darker, substantially cooler, and less saturated, turning the original warm continuation into a grey, relatively detached section.”

From Noemeo’s answer; the whole reply is below

The photographer had seen the darkening, but not the lost saturation: in Lightroom Classic a new mask doesn’t always start from neutral settings, and this one had turned saturation down too. Noemeo’s answer sent the photographer back to the sliders, and two more edits followed.

Strong: coherent, engaging, and substantially resolved, with qualities that sustain attention beyond competent execution.

What works
The upward viewpoint turns the intersecting vaults into an enveloping architectural experience, with sweeping ribs carrying the eye toward the central dome. Warm gold on the left, pale stone through the center, and dark organ pipes on the right give the intricate ornament a readable hierarchy. Detail remains clear enough to reward exploration without losing the larger sensation of grandeur.
What holds it back
The bright windows along the bottom edge pull attention away from the central vault, slightly weakening its role as the visual destination. Otherwise, the main limitation is interpretive rather than technical: the photograph conveys the building’s magnificence convincingly, but offers less of a distinctive emotional perspective beyond awe at its ornament and scale.
Leave as it is
  • Keep the pronounced convergence of the columns and arches: it expresses the sensation of looking overhead and makes the architecture feel enveloping.
  • Keep the contrast between the warmer left-hand vault and the cooler central stonework, which separates the architectural spaces and prevents the ornament from becoming visually uniform.
Edits to try
  • Gently reduce the brightness of the bottom-edge windows and their immediate surrounds to keep attention circulating around the central dome; this would slightly reduce the sense of daylight flooding the interior, so avoid making the windows gray.

New version

Photographer

Reduced bottom brightness. Is it better, or is it too much?

Noemeo

Compared with the version you started with: further from what you were going for.

The reduction is clearly present, and the current version handles the bright bottom windows better: they no longer pull as insistently from the central dome, so the tonal change itself moves the image closer to the assessment’s concern. It is not too dark in a clipping or blocked-shadow sense.

But the adjustment reaches too far beyond brightness. The lower central vault is about 15 L* darker, substantially cooler, and less saturated, turning the original warm continuation into a grey, relatively detached section. That weakens the warm-left/cool-center relationship and the sense of light carrying through the architecture. The extra local contrast near the bottom also makes that area busier, while the section immediately above has become slightly softer.

I’d keep some reduction at the windows, but restore much of the warmth and brightness in the surrounding vault. In short: the aim helped; the present mask is too broad and too chromatically heavy.

A real session

Perugia: is the new version better?

One photo of Perugia, seven versions. Before each answer about a new version, Noemeo measures on your computer what the new version changed: tone, colour, local edits, contrast and fine detail. So when the photographer only asks “Better?”, it can say where the edit landed, say plainly when a change is too small to register, and keep its advice tied to what the photographer is after: perspective.

The outline marks where the details come from. The edits are local and small: enlarge a detail and flip between the two versions to see them.

Noemeo

Noemeo’s first read · Strong

Strong: coherent, engaging, and substantially resolved, with qualities that sustain attention beyond competent execution.

What works
The sunlit church anchors the town beneath a sweeping cloud, giving the photograph a clear relationship between human scale and open sky. Overlapping tiled roofs establish depth, while the distant mountains soften into blue haze. Warm stone against cool sky feels inviting and expansive; the carefully held cloud highlights and textured foreground shadows keep that sense of place tangible.
What holds it back
The church is readable, but the large apartment blocks at left compete with it through their bright facades and repeated windows, making this more an attractive town panorama than a distinctive architectural portrait. The light reveals the scene beautifully without creating a particularly unusual moment. Four faint dust spots are present in plain areas, mostly noticeable only under magnification.
Leave as it is
  • Keep the substantial sky: the cloud's breadth gives the town scale and supplies the photograph's expansive feeling.
  • Keep the deep foreground shadows, whose retained texture provides depth without making every roof compete equally for attention.

New version

Photographer

Do you like it better?

Noemeo

Compared with the version you started with: further from what you were going for.

No—the original works better. The current edit preserves the expansive sky, cloud highlights, and overall colour, but it darkens the lower-left foreground and reduces detail across the lower half. That makes the roofs and façades softer and heavier, weakening the layered depth that made the town feel tangible; the church does not gain enough separation to offset that loss. Nothing appears materially clipped, so this is about tonal weight and texture rather than lost data. I’d return most of the lower-half softening and shadow darkening toward the original, while leaving the sky as it is.

Photographer

I wanted to show a perspective

Noemeo

That helps: if the aim is to show perspective through the overlapping roofs and the town receding toward the mountains, the current edit moves against it slightly. Darkening and softening the lower half compresses the near-to-far layering; the original foreground texture gave the eye more footholds before moving toward the church and distant landscape. I’d keep the foreground shaded, but restore some local definition rather than brighten it broadly. The substantial sky still helps by setting the town’s scale.

Comparing two photos

Two frames of the same causeway: which one?

Noemeo

A is tighter and more destination-driven: the larger fortress gives the causeway a stronger payoff, though the path becomes a visually heavy foreground slab. B steps back, making the exposed tidal landscape feel broader and lonelier; its stronger left-hand rocks add depth, while the orange buoy near the right edge pulls attention sharply. A’s brighter clouds feel more open, whereas B’s darker overhead cloud gives the scene greater austerity. With no stated purpose yet, these are two distinct emphases rather than a clear winner.

Key differences

  • A frames the fortress substantially larger, while B makes it a smaller element within a broader tidal landscape.
  • A’s causeway enters from the lower right; B places it nearer center and shows more of its curve.
  • B includes stronger reddish foreground rocks on the left, creating more texture and depth than A’s smoother mudflats.
  • A has brighter, broken clouds near the horizon; B carries a darker overhead cloud layer and mood.
Photographer

Which image is more eye-catching?

Noemeo

Favours A

Shot A is more immediately eye-catching. Its larger, clearer fortress provides a stronger visual payoff, and the brighter sky separates the landmark more decisively. B has richer foreground texture and a moodier atmosphere, but its smaller fortress and broad terrain distribute attention more diffusely; the orange buoy also competes with the intended subject.

Photographer

It is a story of a tide

Noemeo

Favours B

For a story about the tide, Shot B serves the idea better. Its wider field of exposed rocks, pools, channels, and wet sand makes the retreating sea—not merely the fortress—the subject. The longer causeway reinforces the revealed passage across the seabed. The orange buoy remains distracting, so I would remove or subdue it; a modest sky crop could also give the tidal textures more authority.

Works with Lightroom Classic

Review photos straight from your catalog

  • Review selected photos from the Library menu and keep the critique alongside each photo.
  • Suggested ratings and flags are only ever suggestions — Noemeo asks before changing anything, and you can undo it.
  • Suggest descriptive keywords for your photos.
  • Lightroom is optional: you can also add photos to Noemeo directly.

Privacy

Your photos are stored only on your computer

Your photos, conversations and everything Noemeo learns about them are kept on your own computer — not in Noemeo's cloud.

Reviewing a photo does mean sending it out: for each review, Noemeo sends that photo and the text it needs to an AI model, through Amazon Web Services. It is processed for that request and not stored. Noemeo's cloud keeps only what an account needs: your sign-in, the names of the computers you use Noemeo on, and how much of your allowance you've used.

Availability

Noemeo is in closed beta

The beta is free, covers reviews of 20 photos, and is open to a limited number of photographers. Noemeo Workspace runs on macOS for now; Windows support is planned.

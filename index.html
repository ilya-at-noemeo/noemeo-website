How it works

  1. Review

    A verdict on craft and impact: what works, what holds it back, edits to try.

  2. Talk it through

    Tell it what you were going for; its advice follows your aim. You can see and remove anything it remembers about a photo.

  3. Compare versions

    Export a new version from any editor. On your computer, Noemeo measures what changed in the picture itself — tone, colour, local edits, detail — and says whether it got closer. It says plainly when a change is too small to register.

Any editor; a shortcut for Lightroom Classic

Noemeo works from the exported photo, so any editor will do. Lightroom Classic users can also send photos from the catalog and keep each critique beside the photo. Suggested ratings and flags are applied only when you agree; suggested keywords are added (or replace Noemeo’s earlier ones) when you run that command. You can undo either.

Your library stays on your computer

Your photos, conversations and everything Noemeo learns about them are kept on your computer. For each request (a review, a reply in the conversation, a comparison of versions, keywords), the photos and text it needs pass through Noemeo’s service on Amazon Web Services, which doesn’t store them, to the AI model’s provider (during the beta, OpenAI or Anthropic), which may keep them for a limited period but doesn’t use them for training. Noemeo’s cloud keeps only your sign-in, the names of your computers and when each was last used, how much of your allowance you’ve used and, for 30 days, a log of each request: its time, size, type and outcome, your IP address and an account ID, never its content.
